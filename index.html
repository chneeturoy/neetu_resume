Thinking Outside the Box

Joe Houghton asked us to prove that project management is dynamic. Let's look at how PM principles apply outside the corporate sphere.

💡 The Universal Nature of PM: The Gucci Timetable

Think project management is only for corporate software rollouts? Think again. Scheduling, resource allocation, and stakeholder management apply everywhere—even at home.

Case Study: Daily Operations Management

To demonstrate agile scheduling and resource dependency, I map out a daily operational framework for managing a high-priority, highly demanding stakeholder: my pet dog, Gucci.

The Scope:
• Procurement & Input: Coordinating exact meal timelines (Oats, Ladoos, Omelets) to maintain optimal energy levels.
• KPIs: Monitoring daily hydration goals and entertainment metrics.
• Risk Management: Contingency planning for unexpected variables (e.g., vet visits).

Conclusion: Managing a pet requires the exact same strict timeline adherence and risk mitigation as deploying a small business initiative.

📊 Technical Risk: The Decision Tree Approach

For charities making tough financial choices, a simple risk register isn't always enough. I employ quantitative technical analysis to drive decisions.

The Tool: Cumulative Probability Excel Models

Instead of guessing, I build complex decision trees and cumulative probability tables in Excel. This allows stakeholders to visualize the exact mathematical probability of a project succeeding before committing funds.

The Core PM Framework

Synthesising academic theory (PMBOK, Agile) with real-world application for Irish SMEs.

1. Strategy & Governance

Aligning the project with the charity's mission. Clear governance (like a Steering Committee) prevents scope creep and ensures board accountability.

2. Planning & Implementation

Moving from a Business Case to a Work Breakdown Structure (WBS). Execution requires constant monitoring using tools like Kanban or Gantt charts.

3. Stakeholders & Teams

Blending paid staff with volunteers in Ireland requires specific leadership styles, such as Servant Leadership, to maintain morale and manage expectations.

4. Ethics & Sustainability

Adhering to the Charities Regulator guidelines while ensuring project outcomes meet long-term environmental and social goals (SDGs).
