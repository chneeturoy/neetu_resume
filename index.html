The 12 Core Knowledge Modules

Simplified concepts, visual guides, and tested links to help you manage work without needing a business degree.

🎯 1. Planning & Scope

Imagine planning a massive charity gala. It feels overwhelming. The secret is the "Work Breakdown Structure" (WBS). It simply means chopping a huge goal into tiny, daily chunks until the work feels easy.

Tip: Never start a project without defining what "Done" looks like.
Read: The Layman's Guide to WBS ↗

💬 2. Stakeholder Comm

A "Stakeholder" is just anyone who cares about your project (donors, volunteers, the community). You need a plan to keep them in the loop. A monthly email update can save you hours of answering questions.

Tip: Donors want details; volunteers want clear instructions.
Read: Stakeholder Analysis ↗

🚀 3. Implementation

This is where the actual work happens! The easiest way to track this without fancy software is a Kanban Board. Think of it as a to-do list on steroids.

Just draw three columns on a whiteboard: To Do, Doing, Done. Write tasks on sticky notes and move them across the board as you work. It’s highly visual and incredibly satisfying for the team.

Try Trello (Free Kanban Tool) ↗

🛤️ 4. Methodologies

Don't let the word "methodology" scare you. It just means "how we prefer to work." There are two main flavors:

  • Waterfall: Like building a house. You can't put the roof on until the walls are up. Great for strict government grants.
  • Agile: Like cooking a soup. You taste it, add salt, taste it again, and adjust. Great for event planning and marketing where things change quickly.

🤖 5. AI in Project Management

You don't need to be a tech wizard to use Artificial Intelligence. Tools like ChatGPT can act as your free, personal assistant, saving hours of paperwork for small charities.

Try copy-pasting this prompt into ChatGPT:

"Act as an expert project manager. I am running a local bake sale in Dublin to raise funds for an animal shelter. Create a simple 4-week checklist of things I need to do."

🤝 6. Teams & Leadership

Leading volunteers is very different from leading paid corporate staff. You cannot boss them around. You must use "Servant Leadership"—which simply means asking your team, "What is blocking your work today, and how can I fix it for you?"
